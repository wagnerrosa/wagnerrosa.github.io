<Lead>

Carburante is an iPhone app for motorcycle riders: log a fill-up in three steps, see what the bike really gets per litre, know when the next service is due. I defined it, designed it and built it, and it has been on the App Store in Brazil since 7 October 2026.

<Beat n="01" label="Problem" title={["One wrong digit spoils the average."]}>

I started from my own riding and from conversations with friends and other riders about how they keep track of their bikes: fuel, spend, oil changes. They used spreadsheets, notes and apps built for cars.

At a petrol station a rider has a helmet in one hand and a phone in the other, so logging has to cost almost nothing. And one wrong entry spoils every average, so what the app shows back has to be honest.

<Thesis lines={["Fast in.", "Honest out."]}>Three decisions answer the two halves: friction, trust and honesty.</Thesis>

</Beat>

<Beat n="02" label="Process" title={["Three builds to find the quiet."]}>

The first build was native and correct, and average consumption was one grey row among twelve. The second borrowed from Health and gained hierarchy, and weight. The third kept one hero number.

**Calm is subtraction.** Colour is scarce and carries meaning: a thirsty tank is information, so comparisons stay grey, and red means error or overdue.

<Beat n="03" label="Friction" title={["Three steps.", "The phone does the rest."]}>

Odometer, amount and litres, review. Date, time and place come from the phone, and the fuel type is the last one used.

The first km/l needs two full tanks, so until then the Summary counts down the tanks still missing instead of showing an empty chart.

<Beat n="04" label="Trust" title={["OCR suggests.", "The rider decides."]}>

Apple's Vision framework reads the odometer or the receipt on the phone and pre-fills the fields. In my own tests it also misread: a 27,119 km odometer came back as 71,190, and a pump total of R$ 28.49 as 2,849.

So the review is mandatory, and the interface makes errors loud.

<Beat n="05" label="Honesty" title={["A gap breaks", "the measurement."]}>

Consumption is the kilometres between two full tanks divided by every litre added in between; dividing entry by entry is simpler, and wrong. Forget a few fill-ups and the kilometres are true while the litres are not.

So a gap breaks the measurement: the next full tank becomes a new anchor, and only consumption skips the stretch. **I chose a missing number over a plausible lie.**

</Beat>

<Beat n="06" label="Colour" title={["The bike's colour.", "The system's colour."]}>

The accent follows the manufacturer of the active bike, ten brands in all. The rider's identity is the motorcycle, not my logo.

Harley and KTM orange collides with “due soon”, so state colour never comes from the theme: grey on track, orange from 80% of the interval, red when overdue.

<Beat n="08" label="Habit" title={["A logbook only works as a habit."]}>

**25 achievements and a 10-level profile are on the App Store.** Backdated history never earns a medal, so a medal always means something the rider did. With Premium, an earned medal can become the app icon.

**In TestFlight, not on the store:** group challenges, a private scoreboard for friends, adapted from GymRats. There is no usage data on them yet.
